# Task 3: Traffic Visualisation Interpretations

## Figure 1: Weekday vs Weekend Traffic by Hour

Weekday traffic shows a pronounced time-of-day pattern, with substantially higher traffic during commuting periods than on weekends. The highest average weekday traffic volume occurs at 16:00, reaching approximately 6,240.9, while weekend traffic peaks later at 13:00 at approximately 4,407.6. The weekday profile also shows a strong morning peak, whereas weekend traffic increases more gradually through the morning. This suggests that weekday traffic demand is more strongly influenced by regular commuting patterns.

## Figure 2: Distribution of Hourly Traffic Volume

Hourly traffic volume varies widely, ranging from 0 to 7,280, with a mean of approximately 3,290.7 and a median of 3,427.0. The first and third quartiles are 1,248.5 and 4,952.0 respectively. The histogram is not normally distributed and shows concentrations at both lower and higher traffic volumes, consistent with substantial variation in traffic demand across different times of day.

## Figure 3: Temperature vs Traffic Volume

The scatter plot does not show a strong linear relationship between temperature and traffic volume. Across temperatures ranging from approximately -29.8°C to 36.9°C, traffic volumes remain widely dispersed. The Pearson correlation coefficient is 0.139, indicating only a weak positive linear relationship. This suggests that temperature alone explains relatively little of the variation in traffic volume.

## Figure 4: Congestion Category Distribution by Hour

Congestion levels show a strong time-of-day pattern. Low congestion is most dominant overnight, reaching 100% of observations at 03:00. Medium congestion is most prominent later in the evening, reaching approximately 93.9% at 20:00. High congestion reaches its greatest proportion at 10:00, accounting for approximately 85.8% of observations, while Severe congestion peaks at 16:00, where approximately 70.1% of observations fall into the highest congestion category. These results demonstrate a clear relationship between congestion level and hour of day, with the most severe traffic conditions concentrated during daytime and peak travel periods and substantially lower congestion overnight.
